1. The Four Pillar Expense Scenarios

Every business expense transaction falls into one of four core stages. Understanding the double entry rules ensures correct financial reporting.

01 During Financial Year

Actual Payment of Expense

When a business pays cash or cheque for a service used (e.g., utility bills or staff salaries), money leaves the bank account, and the expense is recorded immediately.

Dr Expense (+ Expense)
Cr Cash at Bank (- Asset)
02 Accrual Basis Theory

Expense Payable (Accruals)

Services consumed during the current year but unpaid by 31 December must still be recorded as expenses incurred for this year, creating an obligation owed to creditors.

Dr Expense (+ Expense)
Cr Expense Payable (+ Current Liability)
03 Matching Theory

Prepaid Expense (Prepayments)

When a business pays in advance for future months, the unused portion belongs to next year and must be removed from the current year's expense balance.

Dr Prepaid Expense (+ Current Asset)
Cr Expense (- Expense)
04 Year-End Closing

Transfer to Income Summary

At year-end, the net incurred expense balance is closed out to the Income Summary account to match against revenues. The expense account balance resets to zero.

Dr Income Summary Account
Cr Expense Account (- Expense)

The Expense Lifecycle Workflow

Follow how money, invoices, and year-end adjustments flow through the accounting ledger.

Step 1

Cash Outflow

Cheque/Bank Transfer paid for utility bill or rent during the financial year.

Entry: Dr Expense / Cr Bank
Step 2

Accrual Audit

Check for services used before Dec 31 that remain unpaid. Add to incurred total.

Entry: Dr Expense / Cr Payable
Step 3

Prepayment Cutoff

Identify payments extending into next financial year. Remove from current total.

Entry: Dr Prepaid / Cr Expense
Step 4

Income Summary

Transfer net incurred expense to Income Summary. Reset ledger balance to $0.

Entry: Dr Income Summary / Cr Expense

Scenario: Tan & Co. Utilities

During the year ended 31 Dec 20X1, Tan & Co. paid $4,200 for utilities via bank transfer. On 31 Dec 20X1, the business owed $300 for December utilities.

Step 1: Identify Figures
• Cash Paid = $4,200
• Ending Payable = $300
• Ending Prepaid = $0
Step 2: Apply Formula
Expense Incurred = $4,200 + $300 - $0 = $4,500
Step 3: Financial Statement
Report $4,500 under Other Expenses in Statement of Financial Performance.
Scenario: Rental Expense

A business reported a total Rental Expense of $24,000 in its Statement of Financial Performance. On 31 Dec 20X1, the business had prepaid rent of $4,000. There was no rent payable.

Step 1: Rearrange Formula
Cash Paid = Expense Incurred + Ending Prepaid
Step 2: Calculate
Cash Paid = $24,000 + $4,000 = $28,000
Step 3: Conclusion
The business paid $28,000 cash/cheque during the year.
Scenario: Swift Logistics Insurance

Paid $10,000 for insurance via cheque. At year-end (31 Dec 20X1), actual insurance expense incurred was determined to be $8,000.

Step 1: Compare Cash vs Expense
$10,000 Cash > $8,000 Incurred (Overpaid!)
Step 2: Calculate Difference
Prepaid = $10,000 - $8,000 = $2,000
Step 3: Presentation
Report $2,000 Prepaid Insurance as Current Asset in Balance Sheet.
