1. The Four Pillar Expense Scenarios

Every business expense transaction falls into one of four core stages. Understanding the double entry rules ensures correct financial reporting.

💡
Crucial POA Rule: At the end of a financial year, a single expense account will have EITHER an Expense Payable (Accrual) OR a Prepaid Expense (Prepayment)—never both simultaneously!
01 During Financial Year

Actual Payment of Expense

When a business pays cash or cheque for a service used (e.g., utility bills or staff salaries), money leaves the bank account, and the expense is recorded immediately.

Dr Expense (+ Expense)
Cr Cash at Bank (- Asset)
02 Accrual Basis Theory

Expense Payable (Accruals)

Services consumed during the current year but unpaid by 31 December must still be recorded as expenses incurred for this year, creating an obligation owed to creditors.

Dr Expense (+ Expense)
Cr Expense Payable (+ Current Liability)
03 Matching Theory

Prepaid Expense (Prepayments)

When a business pays in advance for future months, the unused portion belongs to next year and must be removed from the current year's expense balance.

Dr Prepaid Expense (+ Current Asset)
Cr Expense (- Expense)
04 Year-End Closing

Transfer to Income Summary

At year-end, the net incurred expense balance is closed out to the Income Summary account to match against revenues. The expense account balance resets to zero.

Dr Income Summary Account
Cr Expense Account (- Expense)

Scenario: Tan & Co. Utilities

During the year ended 31 Dec 20X1, Tan & Co. paid $4,200 for utilities via bank transfer. On 31 Dec 20X1, the business owed $300 for December utilities. There were no prepayments.

Step 1: Identify Figures
• Cash Paid = $4,200
• Ending Expense Payable = $300
• Ending Prepaid Expense = $0
Step 2: Apply Master Formula
Expense Incurred = $4,200 + $300 - $0 = $4,500
Step 3: Statement Presentation
• Report $4,500 under Other Expenses (Statement of Financial Performance).
• Report $300 under Current Liabilities (Statement of Financial Position).

Visualizing Utilities Adjustment (Accrual Addition)

The unpaid $300 liability is added to the $4,200 cash payment to reflect the total $4,500 utility service consumed.

Scenario: Rental Expense

A business reported a total Rental Expense of $24,000 in its Statement of Financial Performance. On 31 Dec 20X1, the business had prepaid rent of $4,000. There was no rent payable.

Step 1: Rearrange Formula
Cash Paid = Expense Incurred + Ending Prepaid
Step 2: Calculate Cash Outflow
Cash Paid = $24,000 + $4,000 = $28,000
Step 3: Statement Presentation
• Report $24,000 under Other Expenses (Statement of Financial Performance).
• Report $4,000 under Current Assets (Statement of Financial Position).

Visualizing Rent Prepayment Cutoff

The $28,000 cash paid included $4,000 for future months, leaving exactly $24,000 recognized as current year expense.

Scenario: Swift Logistics Insurance

Paid $10,000 for insurance via cheque during 20X1. At year-end (31 Dec 20X1), the actual insurance expense incurred was determined to be $8,000.

Step 1: Compare Cash vs Expense
$10,000 Cash Paid > $8,000 Incurred (Overpaid!)
Step 2: Calculate Prepaid Asset
Prepaid = $10,000 - $8,000 = $2,000
Step 3: Statement Presentation
• Report $8,000 under Other Expenses (Statement of Financial Performance).
• Report $2,000 as Prepaid Insurance under Current Assets.

Insurance Outflow Asset/Expense Allocation

The $10,000 cash payment splits into an $8,000 consumed expense and a $2,000 prepaid asset carried forward.
