Trade Discount vs. Cash Discount

A Scaffolded Interactive Guide for Principles of Accounts (POA) Students

Understanding the Two Discounts

Businesses offer discounts for two completely different reasons: buying in bulk vs. paying quickly.

⏱️ The Two-Step Transaction Timeline

Follow the story of a business transaction to see when each discount happens:

1
Point of Purchase / Sale (Day 1 - Invoice Issued)
Seller offers a Trade Discount to encourage bulk purchase or customer loyalty. The price tag is reduced immediately on the invoice.
Formula List Price - Trade Discount = Net Invoice Price
2
Point of Payment (e.g., Day 5 - Settlement)
Seller offers a Cash Discount to reward the buyer for paying quickly within a credit period (e.g., within 7 days).
Formula Net Invoice Price - Cash Discount = Actual Cash Paid

🏷️ Trade Discount

Bulk / Loyalty
  • Purpose: Encourage bulk buying or trade loyalty.
  • When: At the time of sale/purchase.
  • Calculated On: Catalog / List Price.
  • Accounting Rule: NEVER RECORDED in account books. Only the Net Invoice Price is entered into Sales or Purchases accounts.

⚡ Cash Discount

Prompt Payment
  • Purpose: Encourage credit customers to pay early.
  • When: At time of payment (if within credit period).
  • Calculated On: Net Invoice Price.
  • Accounting Rule: ALWAYS RECORDED in account books as Discount Allowed or Discount Received.

POA Beginner's Glossary

Click any term to reveal its simple plain-English explanation.

🏷️ List Price +
The original tag price listed in a supplier's catalog before any price reductions or discounts are applied.
📄 Net Invoice Price +
The official agreed price after deducting the trade discount. This is the exact dollar value written on the invoice and recorded in the Sales or Purchases accounts.
🎁 Discount Allowed +
A cash discount given by a seller to a credit customer for paying early. It is classified as an Expense (reduces profit).
💰 Discount Received +
A cash discount enjoyed by a buyer for paying a credit supplier early. It is classified as an Income (increases profit).
👥 Trade Receivable vs Trade Payable +
Trade Receivable (Asset): A credit customer who owes your business money.
Trade Payable (Liability): A credit supplier your business owes money to.
